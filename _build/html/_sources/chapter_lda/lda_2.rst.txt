LDA 2
=====


Probabilistic Latent Semantic Analysis (PLSA) and Latent Dirichlet
Allocation (LDA) are both probabilistic models used for topic modeling,
which is a method to discover abstract topics in a collection of
documents. LDA is considered an improvement over PLSA in several
significant ways.

Generative Process
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

PLSA: PLSA models each document as a mixture of topics, but it does not
have a generative process for documents. The mixture weights are learned
directly from the training data, meaning the model does not naturally
generalize to new, unseen documents. Specifically, PLSA does not define
a probability distribution over the topic mixtures for the entire
collection, making it challenging to predict for new documents.

LDA: LDA introduces a generative process, where each document is assumed
to be generated by first choosing a distribution over topics, drawn from
a Dirichlet distribution. Then each word in the document is generated by
first choosing a topic according to the document’s topic distribution
and then choosing a word according to the topic’s word distribution.
This process allows LDA to define a complete generative model, including
priors, making it possible to generalize to new documents.

Handling New Documents
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

PLSA: The model parameters include a topic distribution for each
document in the training set, but it does not provide a mechanism for
assigning topics to new, unseen documents because it lacks a prior
distribution over topics.

LDA: LDA addresses this limitation by using Dirichlet priors for the
distribution of topics over documents and words over topics. This allows
LDA to infer the topic distribution for new documents using Bayesian
inference.

Parameter Estimation and Regularization
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

PLSA: PLSA is prone to overfitting because it lacks a prior
distribution, and the parameters are estimated directly from the data
without regularization.

LDA: LDA incorporates Dirichlet priors, which act as regularizers. The
Dirichlet prior helps prevent overfitting by smoothing the distribution
of topics over documents and words over topics. This regularization
makes LDA more robust and capable of providing more coherent topics.

Interpretability and Coherence of Topics
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

PLSA: Topics generated by PLSA may not always be as coherent or
interpretable because the model is more prone to overfitting and does
not include a mechanism for encouraging sparsity in the topic
distributions.

LDA: The use of Dirichlet priors in LDA encourages sparsity in the topic
distributions, leading to more interpretable and coherent topics. This
means that each document is typically associated with a small number of
topics, and each topic is characterized by a small number of words with
high probabilities.

LDA Parameter Estimation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), the parameters
:math:`p(w\vert{}z)`, the probability of word :math:`w` given topic
:math:`z`, and :math:`p(z\vert{}d)`, the probability of topic :math:`z`
given document :math:`d`, are estimated through the generative model and
an inference algorithm such as Variational Inference or Gibbs Sampling.

Generative Model
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

LDA’s generative process is:

For each topic :math:`z`, draw a distribution over words :math:`\phi_z`
from a Dirichlet distribution with hyperparameter :math:`\beta`:

.. math:: \phi_z \sim \text{Dir}(\beta)

For each document :math:`d`, draw a distribution over topics
:math:`\theta_d` from a Dirichlet distribution with hyperparameter
:math:`\alpha`:

.. math:: \theta_d \sim \text{Dir}(\alpha)

For each word :math:`w` in document :math:`d`:

Draw a topic :math:`z_{d,n}` from the multinomial distribution
:math:`\theta_d`.

Draw a word :math:`w_{d,n}` from the multinomial distribution
:math:`\phi_{z_{d,n}}`.

The parameters :math:`p(w\vert{}z)` and :math:`p(z\vert{}d)` correspond
to :math:`\phi_z` and :math:`\theta_d`.

1. Estimating :math:`p(w\vert{}z)`

For each topic :math:`z`, :math:`\phi_z` represents the distribution
over words.

Using Gibbs Sampling, an estimate of :math:`\phi_z` can be computed as:

.. math:: \phi_{w\vert{}z} = \frac{n_{w,z} + \beta}{\sum_{w'} (n_{w',z} + \beta)}

where :math:`n_{w,z}` is the count of word :math:`w` assigned to topic
:math:`z` across the corpus, and :math:`\beta` is the Dirichlet prior.

Interpretation:

:math:`\phi_{w\vert{}z}` gives the probability of observing word
:math:`w` given topic :math:`z`.

3. Estimating :math:`p(z\vert{}d)`

For each document :math:`d`, :math:`\theta_d` represents the
distribution over topics.

Using Gibbs Sampling, an estimate of :math:`\theta_d` can be computed
as:

.. math:: \theta_{z\vert{}d} = \frac{n_{z,d} + \alpha}{\sum_{z'} (n_{z',d} + \alpha)}

where :math:`n_{z,d}` is the count of words in document :math:`d`
assigned to topic :math:`z`, and :math:`\alpha` is the Dirichlet prior.

Interpretation:

:math:`\theta_{z\vert{}d}` gives the probability of topic :math:`z`
being present in document :math:`d`.

Summary

In LDA, unlike PLSA, the parameters :math:`p(w\vert{}z)` and
:math:`p(z\vert{}d)` are not directly calculated from data counts alone.
They are inferred as part of a probabilistic model that includes
Dirichlet priors.

Inference can be performed using Variational Inference or Gibbs
Sampling. The Dirichlet priors :math:`\alpha` and :math:`\beta` help
regularize the estimates and allow the model to generalize to new
documents.

The resulting parameters :math:`\phi_z` and :math:`\theta_d` provide the
probabilities of words given topics and topics given documents.

LDA vs PLSA: Formulaic Comparison
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

LDA is an extension and improvement over Probabilistic Latent Semantic
Analysis (PLSA). The main difference is that LDA introduces Dirichlet
priors over the topic distributions.

1. Model Definition and Generative Process

PLSA Model

In PLSA, the probability of a word given a topic is:

.. math:: p(w\vert{}z) = \phi_{w\vert{}z}

where :math:`\phi_{w\vert{}z}` represents the probability of word
:math:`w` given topic :math:`z`.

The probability of a topic given a document is:

.. math:: p(z\vert{}d) = \theta_{z\vert{}d}

where :math:`\theta_{z\vert{}d}` represents the probability of topic
:math:`z` given document :math:`d`.

The joint probability of a word and a document is:

.. math:: p(d,w) = p(d) \sum_z p(z\vert{}d) p(w\vert{}z)

PLSA does not define a prior distribution over
:math:`\theta_{z\vert{}d}`. Therefore, :math:`\theta_{z\vert{}d}` is
only directly defined for documents in the training set.

LDA Model

In LDA, prior distributions are introduced over the document-topic and
topic-word distributions.

Document-topic distribution:

.. math:: \theta_d \sim \text{Dir}(\alpha)

Topic-word distribution:

.. math:: \phi_z \sim \text{Dir}(\beta)

The probability of a word given a topic is:

.. math:: p(w\vert{}z) = \phi_{w\vert{}z}

The probability of a topic given a document is:

.. math:: p(z\vert{}d) = \theta_{z\vert{}d}

The joint probability can be written as:

.. math:: p(d,w,z,\theta_d,\phi_z) = p(d) p(\theta_d\vert{}\alpha) p(z\vert{}\theta_d) p(w\vert{}z,\phi_z)

Formulaic Differences and Implications
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Parameter Estimation and Regularization

PLSA directly estimates :math:`\phi_{w\vert{}z}` and
:math:`\theta_{z\vert{}d}` from data without priors, which can lead to
overfitting.

LDA uses Dirichlet priors :math:`\alpha` and :math:`\beta` over
:math:`\theta_d` and :math:`\phi_z`.

The Dirichlet prior for :math:`\theta_d` is:

.. math:: p(\theta_d\vert{}\alpha) = \frac{\Gamma(\sum_i \alpha_i)}{\prod_i \Gamma(\alpha_i)} \prod_i \theta_{d,i}^{\alpha_i - 1}

The Dirichlet prior for :math:`\phi_z` is:

.. math:: p(\phi_z\vert{}\beta) = \frac{\Gamma(\sum_i \beta_i)}{\prod_i \Gamma(\beta_i)} \prod_i \phi_{z,i}^{\beta_i - 1}

These priors act as regularizers, helping prevent overfitting and
allowing the model to generalize better to new data.

Inference and Generalization

PLSA cannot easily handle new documents because it does not define a
generative process for document-topic distributions.

LDA can infer the topic distribution :math:`\theta_d` for a new document
using the posterior:

.. math:: p(\theta_d\vert{}w_d,\alpha,\beta) \propto p(\theta_d\vert{}\alpha) \prod_{n=1}^{N_d} \sum_{z=1}^K p(z\vert{}\theta_d) p(w_n\vert{}z,\beta)

This is possible because LDA includes the Dirichlet prior :math:`\alpha`
on the document-topic distribution :math:`\theta_d`.

Summary

LDA’s inclusion of Dirichlet priors :math:`\alpha` and :math:`\beta`
provides a more principled framework than PLSA.

The main advantages of LDA over PLSA are:

LDA has a complete generative probabilistic model.

LDA uses Dirichlet priors for regularization.

LDA is better able to generalize to new documents.

LDA can produce sparse and interpretable topic distributions.

LDA is less prone to overfitting than PLSA.

Model Complexity
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

PLSA: PLSA has a simpler model structure without priors on the topic
distributions. The EM algorithm used in PLSA calculates posterior
probabilities :math:`p(z\vert{}d,w)` and updates the model parameters
:math:`p(w\vert{}z)` and :math:`p(z\vert{}d)`. The absence of priors
means fewer parameters and simpler updates.

LDA: LDA introduces Dirichlet priors for both the document-topic
distribution :math:`\theta_d` and the topic-word distribution
:math:`\phi_z`. The inference process, such as Variational Inference or
Gibbs Sampling, requires iterative updates of the variational parameters
or topic assignments. The additional complexity can make LDA
computationally more intensive.

-  Inference Algorithm

PLSA: The EM algorithm used in PLSA generally has straightforward
updates that converge relatively quickly. However, PLSA can suffer from
overfitting and lacks a probabilistic generative process for new
documents.

LDA: Variational Inference in LDA involves optimizing variational
parameters, which can be computationally expensive because of the need
to calculate digamma functions and ensure convergence of the variational
lower bound. Gibbs Sampling involves iteratively sampling topic
assignments for each word, which can also be slow for large datasets.

-  Convergence and Scalability

PLSA: Convergence criteria in PLSA are typically based on changes in the
likelihood function. PLSA may converge faster because fewer parameters
are updated.

LDA: Convergence criteria in LDA involve the variational lower bound or
changes in the latent-variable distributions. The presence of priors and
the need to maintain a coherent generative model can result in slower
convergence, particularly for large datasets.

Final Summary
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

While LDA generally provides a more robust and principled approach to
topic modeling, especially for handling new documents and providing
interpretable topics, it can be computationally slower than PLSA because
of the complexity of the model and the inference algorithms used.

The specific implementation and optimization techniques can
significantly affect the relative speed of both methods. In practice,
efficient implementations or approximations can be used to balance
accuracy and computational efficiency.
